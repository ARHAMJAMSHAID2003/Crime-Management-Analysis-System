The Crime Management Analysis System (CMAS), also known as MehfoozPakistan, is a comprehensive data driven platform designed to modernize crime reporting, investigation tracking, and predictive policing in Pakistan. The primary objective is to provide law enforcement agencies with a centralized system that streamlines crime management while enabling data driven decision making through advanced analytics and pattern detection. Built on a React.js frontend and Node.js/Express backend with Oracle Database integration, CMAS features role based access control for officers, victims, and witnesses, ensuring secure collaboration across all stakeholders. The system manages the complete crime lifecycle from initial reporting and evidence tracking to investigation management and suspect profiling. Its analytical capabilities include crime hotspot identification, trend analysis, and predictive risk assessment using historical data, empowering law enforcement to proactively address crime patterns and enhance operational efficiency for public safety.

Investigation Management with Suspect Tracking and Evidence Chain of Custody
This workflow initiates when an officer accesses their active investigations dashboard after successfully logging into the system with their credentials and JWT token authentication, where they see a comprehensive list of all ongoing investigations assigned to them including case numbers, crime types, dates, locations, current status, and priority levels. The officer selects a specific investigation to work on and the system loads the complete case file displaying all linked crimes, victims, existing evidence, current suspects, witness statements, and investigation timeline. To advance the investigation, the officer begins by adding suspect information through the suspect management module where they can either search the existing Suspect database using filters like name, age, gender, known criminal record, or current status, or create a new suspect record by entering comprehensive details including full name, age, gender, residential address, physical description, known associates, criminal history (marked as 0 for no record or 1 for existing record), and current status (At Large, Arrested, Released, Unknown). Once a suspect is identified, the officer links them to the specific crime by inserting a record into the Crime_Suspect bridge table where they specify the suspect's role (Primary Suspect, Accomplice, Person of Interest), arrest status (Pending, Arrested, Released, Cleared), and any additional notes about the suspect's involvement. The system then allows the officer to manage the evidence collection process by creating Evidence records for each piece of physical or digital evidence found, specifying the evidence type from predefined categories (Fingerprint, Weapon, CCTV Footage, DNA, Document, Digital, Other), writing detailed descriptions of each evidence item, recording who collected it by linking to their Officer_ID, documenting the exact date and time of collection, and most importantly maintaining an unbroken chain of custody by using the sp_update_evidence_chain stored procedure which logs every action performed on the evidence including collection, transfer, analysis, and storage with timestamps and officer information to ensure admissibility in court. As the investigation progresses, the officer adds witness testimonies by creating or linking Witness records, entering their contact information and addresses, recording detailed statements in the Crime_Witness table with statement dates and full text, marking key witnesses with the Is_Key_Witness flag for priority follow-up, and scheduling additional interviews if needed. The officer can also establish connections between related crimes by setting the Related_Crime_ID field when patterns emerge showing the same suspect or modus operandi across multiple incidents, which aids in identifying serial offenders and organized crime networks. Throughout the investigation, the officer regularly updates the investigation status by modifying the Investigation table with current progress notes, adjusting the status field as the case moves through different phases (Active for ongoing work, Suspended if temporarily halted due to lack of leads, Cold Case if no progress for extended period, or Closed when resolved), updating the outcome field when conclusions are reached (Solved when suspect is convicted, Unsolved if case cannot be cracked, No Crime if incident was determined to be accidental or non-criminal), and recording the close date when the investigation concludes. When suspects are arrested, the officer updates their status in both the Suspect table (changing Status to 'Arrested') and in the Crime_Suspect bridge table (updating Arrest_Status), which triggers automatic notifications to all linked victims informing them of the arrest and providing case updates. If the investigation spans multiple crimes, the officer can assign additional team members by adding records to the Investigation_Officer table specifying each officer's role and assignment date, enabling collaborative investigation with clear role definitions. The system provides real-time validation through triggers that prevent deletion of evidence linked to active investigations (trg_maintain_evidence_integrity), automatically validate suspect data entries (trg_validate_suspect_data), and notify when high-priority crimes require immediate attention (trg_notify_high_priority_crime). Upon case closure, the officer conducts a final review ensuring all evidence is properly documented, all suspects are accounted for with final statuses, all victims have been notified of outcomes, witness statements are complete and verified, and investigation notes comprehensively document the entire process from initial report through resolution, after which the system updates all related Crime records to "Closed" status, archives the complete case file for future reference, generates statistical data for analytics, and closes the investigation officially while maintaining complete audit trails for legal and administrative review
